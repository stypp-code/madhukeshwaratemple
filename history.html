History of Madhukeshwara Temple

Historical context for Banavasi, the Kadambas and the Madhukeshwara shrine.

Banavasi was a principal centre of the early Kadambas. An Archaeological Survey of India account records the town’s importance under the Kadambas and later Chalukyas, and notes inscriptions associated with the temple and its precinct. The surviving shrine is not attributable to one construction date: archaeological writing describes an older core and additions and renovation under later dynasties.

Early centuries CE — Excavations in Banavasi document early historic occupation and brick religious structures in the wider town.

Kadamba period — Banavasi served as a major Kadamba capital; historical inscriptions preserve names of Kadamba rulers.

Chalukya and later periods — The temple complex developed over time; the ASI account records a grant dated 1068 CE and later Vijayanagara records of 1368 and 1399.

Sonda period — The same account associates a carved stone festival cot with a Sonda ruler and dates it probably to 1628 CE.

These dates refer to historical evidence and individual objects or records; they should not be read as a definitive date for the entire present temple.

Sources and interpretation

See the ASI volume on temples in the Kanarese districts and the archaeological report on excavations at Banavasi. Both are older publications and are cited as historical sources, not as current visitor guidance.
