# Shri Madhukeshwara Temple, Banavasi — research record

**Research checked:** 7 November 2026  
**Scope:** Historical and visitor-facing content for a website concept. This is desk research, not an official temple statement.  
**Naming:** Sources use Madhukeshwara, Madhukesvara and Madhukesvara/Madhukēśvara. Site UI uses “Madhukeshwara”; source titles retain their spelling.

## Verified facts used on the site

- The temple is in Banavasi, Uttara Kannada district, Karnataka, and is dedicated to Shiva as Madhukeshwara. Karnataka Tourism’s Banavasi destination page describes the temple as a Shiva shrine and the linga as honey-coloured.
- Banavasi was a principal capital of the early Kadambas. The Archaeological Survey of India’s historical account states that Banavasi was a chief capital of the early Kadambas and discusses its later Chalukya history.
- The temple complex has developed across periods. The archaeological literature describes possible Kadamba features in the sanctum and additions/renovation under later rulers. Avoid saying that the entire present temple was built in one year or by one dynasty.
- The ASI account refers to a temple grant dated 1068 CE and Vijayanagara inscriptions dated 1368 and 1399. A carved stone festival cot has an inscription associated with a Sonda ruler; the volume gives its date as probably 1628 CE.
- The ASI excavation paper documents older brick religious structures and early historic occupation in the wider Banavasi archaeological landscape. These remains should not be conflated with the Madhukeshwara shrine.
- Karnataka Tourism describes Kadambotsava as an annual state-level cultural festival honouring Kadamba heritage, with cultural performances in Banavasi. The website does not present it as a temple-administered religious festival.
- Wikimedia Commons identifies the Madhukesvara Temple as ASI monument N-KA-D264. Do not turn this identifier into a claim about the temple’s current managing authority.

## Historical outline

| Period / date | Evidence and cautious interpretation |
|---|---|
| Early historic centuries CE | Archaeological excavation reports document occupation and brick religious structures in Banavasi. These findings concern the wider town/site. |
| Early Kadamba period | Banavasi is identified in the ASI account as a chief capital of the early Kadambas. |
| 634–635 CE | The ASI historical volume cites the Aihole inscription as the earliest inscriptional mention of Banavasi known to that author. This is not claimed as the town’s founding date. |
| 1068 CE | A grant inscription at the Madhukeshwara precinct is reported in the ASI volume. |
| 1368 and 1399 CE | Vijayanagara inscriptions are reported at the temple. |
| Probably 1628 CE | The ASI volume interprets the inscription on a carved stone cot as a Sonda-period gift; the date is explicitly stated as probable. |
